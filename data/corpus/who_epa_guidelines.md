# WHO & EPA Water Quality Guidelines

## WHO Drinking Water Quality Standards (2022)

The World Health Organization (WHO) sets international guidelines for safe drinking water. Key parameters relevant to AquaWatch monitoring:

### Turbidity
- **Guideline:** < 1 NTU at point of delivery (WHO 2022)
- **Treatment trigger:** > 4 NTU requires review
- **Risk:** High turbidity protects pathogens from disinfectants; chlorination is less effective above 1 NTU

### Cyanobacteria / Harmful Algal Blooms (HABs)
WHO risk levels for cyanobacterial blooms in recreational and drinking water:

| Risk Level | Cell Density | Chl-a concentration |
|---|---|---|
| Low risk | < 20,000 cells/mL | < 10 µg/L |
| Moderate risk | 20,000 – 100,000 cells/mL | 10 – 50 µg/L |
| High risk | > 100,000 cells/mL | > 50 µg/L |
| Very high risk | > 10,000,000 cells/mL | > 2,000 µg/L |

**Note:** AquaWatch satellite chlorophyll is a relative index, not a concentration. Ground sampling and lab analysis is required to obtain cell counts or µg/L values.

### pH
- **Guideline:** 6.5 – 8.5 (not directly measured by AquaWatch)

### Nitrates
- **Guideline:** < 50 mg/L NO₃⁻ (not directly measured by AquaWatch)

## EPA Water Quality Criteria (USA)

### Turbidity in Surface Waters
- **EPA 304(a) criteria:** Context-dependent. Recreational use impairment flagged at > 25 NTU above background.
- **Drinking water source protection:** Source water with turbidity > 10 NTU triggers enhanced treatment.

### Chlorophyll-a Thresholds
EPA nutrient criteria for lakes (varies by ecoregion):

| Trophic State | Chl-a (µg/L) |
|---|---|
| Oligotrophic (clear) | < 2 µg/L |
| Mesotrophic | 2 – 8 µg/L |
| Eutrophic | 8 – 25 µg/L |
| Hypereutrophic | > 25 µg/L |

### Total Phosphorus & Eutrophication
- Eutrophication is driven by nutrient loading (phosphorus, nitrogen)
- Symptoms include elevated chlorophyll, low transparency, algal scum
- AquaWatch detects eutrophication symptoms via chl-a and transparency indicators

## When to Trigger a Public Health Response

AquaWatch alerts are decision support tools — not regulatory triggers. Standard practice:

1. **Watch alert (1σ–2σ):** Increase monitoring frequency. No immediate action required.
2. **Warning alert (2σ–3σ):** Send field team. Collect water samples. Send to certified lab.
3. **Severe alert (>3σ):** Consider precautionary measures (boil water advisory, intake shutdown review) PENDING lab confirmation.
4. **Never issue public health advisories based solely on satellite data.**

## Disclaimer

All AquaWatch indicators are relative satellite measurements derived from optical remote sensing. They are NOT equivalent to laboratory measurements of pollutant concentrations. Ground sampling and certified laboratory analysis is required before any regulatory, public health, or operational decision.
