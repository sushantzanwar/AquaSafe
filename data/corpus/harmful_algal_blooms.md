# Harmful Algal Blooms (HABs) — Science & Response Guide

## What Are Harmful Algal Blooms?

Harmful Algal Blooms (HABs) occur when cyanobacteria (blue-green algae) or other algae multiply rapidly and accumulate in water bodies. Some species produce toxins (cyanotoxins) that are dangerous to humans, animals, and aquatic ecosystems.

## Common Cyanotoxins

| Toxin | Produced by | Primary Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Microcystins | *Microcystis*, *Planktothrix* | Liver damage (hepatotoxic); most common |
| Cylindrospermopsins | *Cylindrospermopsis*, *Aphanizomenon* | Liver, kidney, neurological damage |
| Anatoxins | *Anabaena*, *Oscillatoria* | Neurotoxic, can be rapidly fatal to animals |
| Saxitoxins | *Anabaena*, *Aphanizomenon* | Paralytic shellfish poisoning |

## Satellite Detection

AquaWatch detects conditions that are *favourable* for HABs:

- **Elevated chlorophyll-a (relative index):** Indicates phytoplankton biomass accumulation
- **Decreased transparency:** Water clarity drops as algae blocks light
- **Warm, calm surface conditions:** Not directly measured but inferred from context

**Important limitation:** Satellite imagery cannot distinguish between non-toxic and toxic algal species. Visual appearance (colour, scum) and laboratory analysis are required to confirm toxin-producing blooms.

## Bloom Lifecycle

1. **Growth phase:** Nutrients (phosphorus, nitrogen) fuel rapid algal growth. Chlorophyll rises.
2. **Surface accumulation:** Calm winds concentrate scum at the surface. May appear green, blue-green, or rust-coloured.
3. **Decomposition:** Bloom dies, oxygen is depleted (hypoxia/anoxia), fish kills may occur.

## Satellite Indicators of HAB Risk

| AquaWatch Signal | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| Chlorophyll > 2σ | Elevated algal biomass — monitor closely |
| Chlorophyll > 3σ | Possible bloom conditions |
| Turbidity drops while chl-a rises | Algal bloom dominating (not sediment) |
| Transparency declines alongside chl-a | Combined bloom + clarity impact |

## Response Protocol

1. **Visual inspection first:** Field teams should look for scum, discolouration, dead fish, or unusual odour.
2. **Sample collection:** Collect surface and sub-surface water samples in clean containers.
3. **Lab analysis:** Test for cell counts (microscopy), chl-a concentration (spectrophotometry), and toxin levels (ELISA or HPLC).
4. **Public notification:** Only after lab confirmation of toxin levels above regulatory thresholds.

## Health Effects in Humans

- **Skin contact:** Rashes, eye/throat irritation
- **Ingestion (drinking water):** Nausea, vomiting, liver damage, neurological effects
- **Inhalation (spray/aerosols near blooms):** Respiratory irritation
- **Children and pets** are at higher risk due to lower body mass

## Eutrophication — Root Cause

Most HABs are linked to eutrophication — the enrichment of water with excess nutrients, typically from:
- Agricultural runoff (fertilisers — phosphorus, nitrogen)
- Sewage discharge
- Urban stormwater
- Atmospheric deposition

Long-term monitoring with AquaWatch can identify water bodies with increasing eutrophication trends by tracking the chlorophyll baseline over seasons.
